Beams

A Beam consists of a wavelength and the SecondOrderMoments of the beam.

Simple astigmatic beams

Most beams are described by their parameters along two perpendicular principal axes. Each axis is given by one of the subtypes of AxisParameters, and both axes can be described differently:

λ = 1.03e-6
Beam(λ, Waist(1e-3))                                    # round beam at its waist
Beam(λ, Waist(2e-3), Waist(1e-3); φ=deg2rad(30))        # elliptical beam, rotated by 30°
Beam(λ, BeamRadius(1e-3, 0.5), RayleighRange(2.0; z=1.0))
Beam(λ, ComplexBeamParameter(0.1 + 2.0im))
GaussianBeamPropagation.AxisParameters — Type
AxisParameters

Supertype of the descriptions of a Gaussian beam along one of its principal axes, used to construct a Beam:

  • BeamRadius: beam radius and radius of curvature of the phase front,
  • Waist: waist radius and distance from the waist,
  • RayleighRange: Rayleigh range and distance from the waist,
  • ComplexBeamParameter: complex beam parameter q.

New descriptions are added by defining a subtype together with a method of GaussianBeamPropagation.axis_moments(λ, parameters), which returns the second moments (⟨r²⟩, ⟨rθ⟩, ⟨θ²⟩) along that axis for the wavelength λ.

General beams

Beams with general astigmatism are described by their second-order moments, which can be given by the principal axes of the spatial, angular and mixed moments, and the twist:

moments = SecondOrderMoments(;
    spatial=PrincipalMoments(1e-6, 4e-6, 0.1),
    angular=PrincipalMoments(1e-8, 2e-8, 0.4),
    twist=1e-10,
)
Beam(λ, moments)
GaussianBeamPropagation.Beam — Type
Beam(λ, x::AxisParameters, y::AxisParameters=x; φ=0)
Beam(λ, moments::SecondOrderMoments)
Beam(λ, matrix::SMatrix{4,4})

A beam with a wavelength λ, described by its second-order moments.

A simple astigmatic Gaussian beam is given by its parameters along its two principal axes x and y (see AxisParameters), which are rotated by φ relative to the x and y directions. A general beam is given by its SecondOrderMoments, or by the symmetric 4×4 matrix of second-order moments in the order (x, y, θx, θy).

The fields are the wavelength λ and the second-order moments.

A Beam is propagated by multiplying optical elements onto it from the left, and its properties are obtained with, e.g., radius, divergence, phase_curvature and twist.

Examples

# Elliptical beam at its waist, with the principal axes rotated by 30°
beam = Beam(1.03e-6, Waist(2e-3), Waist(1e-3); φ=deg2rad(30))
radius(FreeSpace(0.5) * beam)

GaussianBeamPropagation.SecondOrderMoments — Type
SecondOrderMoments(; spatial::PrincipalMoments, angular::PrincipalMoments,
                     mixed::PrincipalMoments=PrincipalMoments(0.0), twist=0.0)
SecondOrderMoments(rxrx, rxry, ryry, θxθx, θxθy, θyθy, rxθx, rxθy, ryθx, ryθy)
SecondOrderMoments(matrix::SMatrix{4,4})
SecondOrderMoments(beam::Beam)

The second-order moments of a beam: the spatial moments $⟨x²⟩, ⟨xy⟩, ⟨y²⟩$ (rxrx, rxry, ryry), the angular moments $⟨θx²⟩, ⟨θxθy⟩, ⟨θy²⟩$ (θxθx, θxθy, θyθy) and the mixed moments $⟨xθx⟩, ⟨xθy⟩, ⟨yθx⟩, ⟨yθy⟩$ (rxθx, rxθy, ryθx, ryθy).

They can be given directly, as the symmetric 4×4 matrix of moments in the order (x, y, θx, θy), or by the principal axes of the spatial, angular and mixed moments together with the twist, the antisymmetric part of the mixed moments.

The moments do not depend on the wavelength. A beam with a wavelength is a Beam.
