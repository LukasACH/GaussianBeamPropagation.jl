GaussianBeamPropagation.ThickLens — Function
ThickLens(front_surface::Curvature, thickness::Real, back_surface::Curvature, n::Real)
ThickLens(front_surface::Curvature, thickness::Real, back_surface::Curvature, n::Real, incidence::IncidenceAngle)

Matrix representing a thick lens

This is a matrix builder that constructs the matrix for a thick lens.

The arguments are the following:

  • front_surface::Curvature: curvature of the front lens surface given by Curvature, where a positive curvature denotes that the center of curvature lies after the lens, i.e., the front surface is convex.
  • thickness::Real: thickness of the lens where the ray or beam passes through.
  • back_surface::Curvature: curvature of the back given by Curvature, where a positive curvature denotes that the center of curvature lies after the lens, i.e., the back surface is concave.
  • n::Real: ratio of the refractive index of the lens material to the refractive index of the surrounding material.
  • incidence::IncidenceAngle (optional): if given, defines the direction of the incident ray or beam using IncidenceAngle. If absent, it is assumed that the ray is parallel to the surface normal in the center (disregarding any surface curvature).
Coordinate system

The coordinate system used by the matrix is right handed, where the ray or beam travels in the +z direction. Both the orientation angle of the surface and the orientation of the incidence plane are rotations in the x-y-plane relative to the x̂ direction.

GaussianBeamPropagation.fit_propagation — Method
fit_propagation(λ, data; system=free_space_system, kwargs...)

Fit the input beam to beam measurements taken after propagating through free space. This is fit_system with a fixed system and no system parameters.

data has one row per measurement with the columns beam radius along x, beam radius along y, orientation angle and propagation distance. Elements between the measurements can be included by passing system(row, params) with row = [L]. The remaining kwargs are passed on to fit_system.

source
GaussianBeamPropagation.fit_system — Method
fit_system(system, λ, vars, observed; system_params=FitParameters(), beam_params=beam_fit_parameters(λ),
           optimizer=NelderMead(), options=Optim.Options(; g_tol=1e-10, iterations=10000))

Fit the input beam and the parameters of an optical system to measured beam moments.

  • system(row, params) returns the transfer element (anything that can be multiplied onto a Beam) for one measurement: row is a row of vars and params the current system parameters.
  • vars is a matrix with one row of measurement settings per observation (e.g. a distance).
  • observed is the tuple (rxrx, rxry, ryry) of measured moments, see moments_from_radius.
  • system_params holds the bounds and initial values of the system parameters, and beam_params those of the input beam (see beam_fit_parameters).
Bounds

The default optimiser, Nelder-Mead, does not enforce the bounds, and fitted values can leave them. In particular, parameters cannot be fixed by setting equal bounds.

Returns a SystemFit.

GaussianBeamPropagation.principal_axes — Method
principal_axes(xx, xy, yy)

Principal values and orientation (x, y, φ) of the symmetric tensor [xx xy; xy yy], such that tensor_from_principal(x, y, φ) == (xx, xy, yy).

x belongs to the principal axis closest to the x direction, and φ ∈ [−π/4, π/4] is the angle between the two. All quantities given along x and y use this convention.

GaussianBeamPropagation.radius — Method
radius(::Beam [, φ::Real])
radius(::SecondOrderMoments [, φ::Real])
radius(xx, xy, yy [, φ::Real])

Calculates the 1/e² radius from a Beam, SecondOrderMoments or measured second order moments (xx, xy, and yy). The return value is a SVector with three elements, the radius in both x and y, and the angle φ. The values x and y are the given in metres, and the angle φ is given in radians. It follows the definition of ISO 11146 for x, y, and the angle φ: x is the principal axis closest to the x direction, and φ ∈ [−π/4, π/4] is its angle to the x direction.

If the optional argument φ is provided, the radius is calculated along the direction of the angle φ (in radians). In that case, the return value is a SVector with only two elements, the radius in the x and y directions, both given in metres.

It uses a right-handed coordinate system.

GaussianBeamPropagation.sample_beam_continuous — Method
sample_beam_continuous([f=radius,] beams)

Like sample_beam, but follows each principal axis continuously through beams, for example along a propagation distance or a scanned parameter.

The quantities along x and y (see radius) jump whenever the principal axes rotate past ±45°, because x is always the axis closest to the x direction. Here, the axes are swapped back whenever that happens, and the angles are unwrapped, such that x, y, φx and φy are continuous for a continuous sequence of beams.

GaussianBeamPropagation.AxisParameters — Type
AxisParameters

Supertype of the descriptions of a Gaussian beam along one of its principal axes, used to construct a Beam:

  • BeamRadius: beam radius and radius of curvature of the phase front,
  • Waist: waist radius and distance from the waist,
  • RayleighRange: Rayleigh range and distance from the waist,
  • ComplexBeamParameter: complex beam parameter q.

New descriptions are added by defining a subtype together with a method of GaussianBeamPropagation.axis_moments(λ, parameters), which returns the second moments (⟨r²⟩, ⟨rθ⟩, ⟨θ²⟩) along that axis for the wavelength λ.

GaussianBeamPropagation.Beam — Type
Beam(λ, x::AxisParameters, y::AxisParameters=x; φ=0)
Beam(λ, moments::SecondOrderMoments)
Beam(λ, matrix::SMatrix{4,4})

A beam with a wavelength λ, described by its second-order moments.

A simple astigmatic Gaussian beam is given by its parameters along its two principal axes x and y (see AxisParameters), which are rotated by φ relative to the x and y directions. A general beam is given by its SecondOrderMoments, or by the symmetric 4×4 matrix of second-order moments in the order (x, y, θx, θy).

The fields are the wavelength λ and the second-order moments.

A Beam is propagated by multiplying optical elements onto it from the left, and its properties are obtained with, e.g., radius, divergence, phase_curvature and twist.

Examples

# Elliptical beam at its waist, with the principal axes rotated by 30°
beam = Beam(1.03e-6, Waist(2e-3), Waist(1e-3); φ=deg2rad(30))
radius(FreeSpace(0.5) * beam)

GaussianBeamPropagation.Curvature — Type
Curvature(κ::Real)
Curvature(κx::Real, κy::Real; φ::Real)
Curvature(κ_xx::Real, κ_xy::Real, κ_yy::Real)

Spherical curvature

Defines the curvature of a spherical surface from a single curvature κ, which is defined as the reciprocal radius of curvature R, such that κ = R⁻¹. It has a single argument:

  • κ::Real: defines the spherical curvature of the surface.

Toroidal curvature

To define a toroidal curvature (including a cylindrical surface, two radii of curvature in two principal axes are required:

  • κx::Real: defines the surface curvature in the x' direction.
  • κy::Real: defines the surface curvature in the y' direction.
  • φ::Real (keyword, optional): defines the rotation of the x' and y' directions relative to the global x and y directions.

Projected curvature

Instead of providing the curvature in the principal directions with a rotation angle, you can provide the components of the vectors already rotated:

  • κ_xx::Real: projected curvature in the x direction.
  • κ_xy::Real: (κx - κy) * cos(φ) * sin(φ)
  • κ_yy::Real: projected curvature in the y direction
Coordinate system

The coordinate system used by the matrix is right handed, where the ray or beam travels in the +z direction. Both the orientation angle of the surface and the orientation of the incidence plane are rotations in the x-y-plane relative to the x̂ direction.

The curvature κ of a surface is the inverse of its radius of curvature R, such that R ≡ κ⁻¹. It is given in units of inverse metre.

The coordinate system used by the matrix is right handed, where the ray or beam travels in the +z direction. Both the orientation angle of the surface and the orientation of the incidence plane are rotations in the x-y-plane relative to the x̂ direction.

GaussianBeamPropagation.SecondOrderMoments — Type
SecondOrderMoments(; spatial::PrincipalMoments, angular::PrincipalMoments,
                     mixed::PrincipalMoments=PrincipalMoments(0.0), twist=0.0)
SecondOrderMoments(rxrx, rxry, ryry, θxθx, θxθy, θyθy, rxθx, rxθy, ryθx, ryθy)
SecondOrderMoments(matrix::SMatrix{4,4})
SecondOrderMoments(beam::Beam)

The second-order moments of a beam: the spatial moments $⟨x²⟩, ⟨xy⟩, ⟨y²⟩$ (rxrx, rxry, ryry), the angular moments $⟨θx²⟩, ⟨θxθy⟩, ⟨θy²⟩$ (θxθx, θxθy, θyθy) and the mixed moments $⟨xθx⟩, ⟨xθy⟩, ⟨yθx⟩, ⟨yθy⟩$ (rxθx, rxθy, ryθx, ryθy).

They can be given directly, as the symmetric 4×4 matrix of moments in the order (x, y, θx, θy), or by the principal axes of the spatial, angular and mixed moments together with the twist, the antisymmetric part of the mixed moments.

The moments do not depend on the wavelength. A beam with a wavelength is a Beam.
